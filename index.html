Hocbigg - Urban Studies and Planning

Path to a free self-taught education in Urban Studies and Planning.

Last updated on: 2026-10-08

Introduction

Urban studies investigates how cities form, function, and evolve, drawing on sociology, economics, history, and geography to understand urban life. Urban planning is the applied, deliberate practice of intervening in that built and social environment—shaping land use, housing, transportation networks, and public space to balance economic viability, environmental resilience, and social equity. Together, they provide both the analytical lens to diagnose urban challenges and the practical tools to design interventions.

This curriculum is built for independent learners starting with no prior background in design, economics, or public policy. It assumes no institutional affiliation, proprietary software, or academic database access. Foundational concepts are introduced from first principles, and technical methods rely on accessible, open platforms like QGIS.

This guide is designed for individuals seeking a comprehensive, grounded understanding of the discipline rather than an exhaustive academic research track. It focuses on the core conceptual models and real-world mechanisms that planners and urbanists use, bypassing narrow academic debates and specialized research methodologies.

How the Sequence Works

The curriculum is structured into ten core subjects that build logically from broad historical context to specialized physical systems and spatial data methods:

  • Social and Historical Foundations: The opening modules (Introduction to Urban Studies, Urban History and the Evolution of Planning, and Urban Sociology and Community Dynamics) examine the origins of cities, historical urban planning movements, and how neighborhood composition and social inequalities shape daily life.
  • Mechanisms of Planning and Form: The middle modules (Planning Theory and Practice, Urban Design and City Form, Urban Economics, and Land Use Planning, Zoning, and Urban Law) cover how cities are actively regulated and shaped. Here you will learn about the planner's professional role, physical morphology, market forces and land rents, and the legal frameworks governing property and zoning.
  • Infrastructure and Environmental Systems: The next modules (Transportation and Urban Mobility and Urban Sustainability and Environmental Planning) address the networked systems that keep cities functioning, focusing on transit network design, multi-modal access, decarbonization, and climate resilience.
  • Spatial Literacy: The final module (Urban Data, Mapping, and Basic Spatial Analysis) equips you with the practical ability to visualize census data, interpret municipal plans, and perform fundamental geographic information systems (GIS) workflows.

While you can dip into individual topics independently, following the suggested sequence ensures that regulatory, economic, and design concepts are clearly established before you encounter them in transportation or environmental systems.

Scope and Next Steps

This page contains only the essential core subjects that represent the shared baseline of an undergraduate education in urban planning. It intentionally omits specialized career tracks, advanced econometric modeling, and complex development finance.

Once you have worked through these core foundations, you can branch out into specific subfields:

  • Explore specialized tracks—such as housing policy, urban analytics, climate resilience, and transit operations—in Advanced Topics.
  • Apply your skills by creating real-world planning deliverables, such as site analyses, walkability audits, and policy briefs, in Projects.

Curriculum

Introduction to Urban Studies

Examines why cities form, how urbanization shapes social life and global economies, and the primary debates surrounding what makes urban areas thrive.

CitiesX: The Past, Present and Future of Urban Life (edX / HarvardX, Edward Glaeser) - The primary video-based survey for this subject, offering an accessible, interdisciplinary overview of global urbanization that is free to audit.

The City Reader, 7th ed. (Routledge, LeGates & Stout) - A comprehensive anthology of foundational texts in urban studies; read selections that correlate with the weekly topics in CitiesX rather than reading it cover to cover.

Triumph of the City (Edward Glaeser, Penguin Books) - An accessible, single-author narrative alternative to CitiesX covering similar economic and historical principles, suitable for learners who prefer sustained reading over video lectures.

Urban History and the Evolution of Planning

Traces the spatial and social development of cities from ancient origins through industrialization to modern planning movements.

Cities of Tomorrow: An Intellectual History of Urban Planning and Design Since 1880, 4th ed. (Peter Hall, Wiley-Blackwell) - The definitive narrative textbook on modern planning ideas, detailing the social and intellectual movements that responded to the industrial city.

The City in History: Its Origins, Its Transformations, and Its Prospects (Lewis Mumford, Mariner Books) - A seminal classic providing historical depth on the civilizational role of cities from antiquity to the early 20th century; read selected early chapters to establish historical context prior to Hall.

The Once and Future City (MIT OpenCourseWare, Anne Whiston Spirn) - A practical, field-based guide providing structured assignment prompts for "reading" and documenting the historical artifacts and spatial layers of your own local built environment.

Urban Sociology and Community Dynamics

Explores neighborhood structures, social networks, urban inequalities, and the ways physical environments shape community life.

Cities are back in town : urban sociology for a globalizing urban world (Coursera / Sciences Po, Patrick Le Galès) - The core instructional lecture course for this subject, surveying sociological theories from the Chicago School to contemporary urban political economy with global comparative cases; free to audit.

The Death and Life of Great American Cities (Jane Jacobs, Vintage Books) - The canonical, essential text on sidewalk ballet, street-level social cohesion, and the organic vitality of urban neighborhoods; read alongside the Coursera lectures.

Evicted: Poverty and Profit in the American City (Matthew Desmond, Broadway Books) - A contemporary ethnographic study that grounds sociological theories of poverty, housing insecurity, and neighborhood exploitation in vivid real-world detail.

Planning Theory and Practice

Covers the role of the professional planner, decision-making frameworks, procedural ethics, and the political context of public planning interventions.

Contemporary Urban Planning, 12th ed. (John M. Levy, Sonia A. Hirt & Casey J. Dawkins, Routledge) - The standard introductory textbook covering the political frameworks, institutional histories, and everyday practice of comprehensive planning.

Gateway to the Profession of Planning (MIT OpenCourseWare, Bishwapriya Sanyal) - A complementary seminar providing lecture notes and structured debate prompts exploring rational planning, incrementalism, advocacy planning, and professional ethics to deepen Levy's overview.

Urban Design and City Form

Analyzes the physical configuration of the built environment, spatial perception, street networks, and human-scale public spaces.

Theory of City Form (MIT OpenCourseWare, Julian Beinart) - A complete 25-lecture video course examining historical theories and morphological models of city form from antiquity to the present; use this as the visual and conceptual spine.

The Image of the City (Kevin Lynch, MIT Press) - A foundational text introducing cognitive mapping and the five elements of urban form (paths, edges, districts, nodes, and landmarks); read in conjunction with Beinart's lectures.

Life Between Buildings: Using Public Space, 6th ed. (Jan Gehl, Island Press) - A practical, human-scale field guide detailing how street design, sightlines, and edge conditions encourage or inhibit public social activity.

Urban Economics

Investigates market forces shaping cities, agglomeration economies, land-rent gradients, housing submarkets, and local public finance.

Urban Economics, 9th ed. (Arthur O'Sullivan, McGraw-Hill) - The foundational textbook explaining why cities exist, firm clustering, the monocentric city model, and urban growth dynamics (earlier editions are widely available for open digital library borrowing).

Planning Economics (MIT OpenCourseWare, Frank Levy) - An applied open-courseware companion providing lecture notes, problem sets, and solutions that apply microeconomic tools like cost-benefit analysis, externalities, and public goods directly to planning scenarios.

Land Use Planning, Zoning, and Urban Law

Surveys regulatory frameworks, comprehensive planning mechanisms, the police power, zoning ordinances, and property law jurisprudence.

Arbitrary Lines: How Zoning Broke the American City and How to Fix It (M. Nolan Gray, Island Press) - An accessible, critical primer explaining the history, mechanics, and systemic consequences of single-use Euclidean zoning, serving as a readable conceptual on-ramp.

Community Growth and Land Use Planning (MIT OpenCourseWare, Terry Szold) - Provides practical planning tools, project guidelines, and regulatory strategies for managing growth, corridor development, and comprehensive plan drafting.

Legal Aspects of Property and Land Use (MIT OpenCourseWare, Balakrishnan Rajagopal) - Explores the underlying constitutional and common-law doctrines—including takings, eminent domain, and exclusionary zoning—that govern land use regulation.

Transportation and Urban Mobility

Examines street hierarchies, public transit network design, multi-modal systems, and the policy trade-offs inherent in mobility planning.

Human Transit, Revised Edition (Jarrett Walker, Island Press) - An indispensable primer on the geometric and operational principles governing public transit networks, focusing on the trade-offs between ridership and geographic coverage.

Urban Transportation Planning (MIT OpenCourseWare, Frederick Salvucci) - Comprehensive lecture notes providing a masterclass on the political, institutional, and environmental realities of financing and executing metropolitan transport projects.

Urban Sustainability and Environmental Planning

Focuses on climate resilience, ecological infrastructure, circular urban metabolism, and equitable environmental transitions.

Greening the Economy: Sustainable Cities (Coursera / Lund University, Harriet Bulkeley) - A structured foundational MOOC examining urban decarbonization, nature-based solutions, and sustainable infrastructure transitions; free to audit.

Rethink the City: New Approaches to Global and Local Urban Challenges (edX / TU Delft) - An essential complementary course emphasizing spatial justice, informal housing, and climate resilience in the Global South; free to audit.

Planning for Sustainable Development (MIT OpenCourseWare, David Laws & Martin Rein) - A supplementary reading seminar providing deeper policy analysis on local climate action plans and organizational governance.

Urban Data, Mapping, and Basic Spatial Analysis

Builds practical spatial data literacy, map design principles, and desktop GIS workflows for visualizing and analyzing urban demographic and land use patterns.

Maps and the Geospatial Revolution (Penn State / Dutton Institute, Anthony C. Robinson) - A self-paced, open-access course teaching fundamental spatial thinking, coordinate reference systems, and cartographic design principles; start here before touching mapping software.

Geographic Information System (GIS) Tutorial (MIT OpenCourseWare) - A concise, beginner-friendly introduction to software navigation, layer structures, and basic map compilation using either ArcGIS Pro or open-source QGIS.

Introduction to Spatial Analysis (MIT OpenCourseWare, Sarah Williams & Eric Robsky Huntley) - An applied planning laboratory featuring instructional demonstration videos and practical exercises (available for both QGIS and ArcGIS) covering geocoding, proximity buffers, spatial joins, and census data analysis.
